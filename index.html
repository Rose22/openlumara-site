Your AI. Your life.
Your terms.

OpenLumara is a manually written (not vibecoded) personal AI assistant that lives on your computer. Made for everyday people — people who use AI to ask questions, get help with life, summarize articles, and so on. It can code too, but that's optional and not its main purpose.

This is a harness for everyone: everyday users, power users, and developers alike. It remembers your life, sets your reminders, and talks to you wherever you are — with nothing leaving your machine. Built to help you switch off expensive cloud AI services like ChatGPT and Gemini, by making local models perform better and enhancing them with features like web search.

Think of it like a more advanced, fully private, local version of Google Assistant, Siri, Amazon Alexa, and so on. Supercharged by modern AI - but fulfilling many of the same needs. You can dump your entire life into it with confidence - because your data never leaves your device. Prompt injection attacks can't get your data because of the secure way it is all designed - scroll down to learn more.

Security

You're in control. Always.

OpenLumara doesn't trust the AI to police itself. It doesn't rely on "please don't do bad things" — instead, the framework physically blocks anything you haven't allowed. You decide what your AI can see and do, and you can change your mind anytime.

The AI can't decide its own rules

No prompts asking the AI to "behave nicely." The framework sets hard limits, so the AI simply can't do things you haven't allowed — no exceptions.

Turn features on and off like permissions

Enable memory, and your AI remembers. Disable the command line, and it can't use one. Toggle any feature or change its settings, and you instantly change what your AI can do.

Command line, locked away

The command line is off by default. When you turn it on, it runs inside a restricted container with limits on CPU, memory, and processes — and you can cut its internet access too.

Code help, without risks

Your AI checks its own code before saving anything. It has read-only mode, line limits, and blocked folders. It can write code — but it can't damage your system.

Web browsing, controlled

When your AI browses the web, you can force it to use only secure connections, block certain websites, and prevent it from reaching your local network.

Risky features are clearly labeled

Dangerous modules are marked "unsafe" in the settings, so you always know what you're enabling. And system commands like restarting work even if the AI is stuck — they bypass the AI entirely.

Features

Your AI, the way you want it.

OpenLumara is built for your whole life — your memories, your schedule, your daily routines, your coding projects. Every feature is optional, and every feature is under your control. Turn things on when you need them, off when you don't.

Turn features on and off

Want your AI to remember things? Enable memory. Want it to send you reminders? Enable the scheduler. Want to talk to the AI with nothing extra? Turn everything off. It's all yours to decide.

Fast, even with local AI

Your AI only needs to process about 4,000 tokens of setup — that's like two pages of text. The rest of the conversation stays tiny too. You can see exactly how much your AI is processing at any time.

Your data stays yours

No cloud. No account. No company collecting your conversations. Everything runs on your own computer. You can even use free, local AI models that never send your data anywhere.

Works wherever you are

Chat with your AI in your browser, on Telegram, Discord, or right from your terminal. It remembers everything across all of them — one AI, everywhere you are.

Reminders and routines

Set up morning routines, medication reminders, grocery check-ins — recurring or one-time. Your AI wakes up and does its tasks automatically, even when you're not talking to it.

Helps you write code

Your AI can write, edit, and fix code for you — safely. It checks its work before saving anything, and you control exactly what files it can touch. No shell access unless you want it.

Remembers everything

It remembers your preferences, your habits, your past conversations — and it brings the most important things to mind automatically. The rest is searchable when you need it.

Safety built in

By default, your AI can't access your computer's command line, browse the web freely, or do anything risky. You decide what it's allowed to do — and you can change your mind anytime.

System prompt · 4,256 tokens

Everything your AI always needs to know: who you are, what features are active, and your most important memories. Rose22's instance holds more than half a year of memories — and it's still around 4k tokens, because only the most important ones are kept in mind at all times. The rest is stored and searchable when you need it.

Active tools · 7,768 tokens, 74 active

Your AI's capabilities — like web search, calendar, and reminders — are listed separately from the system prompt. This keeps things organized and fast. Rose22's instance has most things turned on. A basic setup with just the essentials uses only 21 tools and 1,877 tokens — much lighter.

Your conversation · 419 tokens

Only the recent chat history. When things get too long, your AI automatically forgets the oldest messages to stay fast. It always keeps your latest message safe.

End prompt (aka "author's note") · 22 tokens

Things that change all the time — like the current time — live at the very end. Updating them never forces your AI to re-read everything else.

You can see exactly how much your AI processes at any time. Ask it to show you, and it will — so you always know what it's paying attention to.

Pi

Why not Pi?

  • →

    Made for developers, not everyday people. Pi's main focus is coding, and it's great at it - but using it for anything else but coding requires a lot of modifications, config file editing, and extensions.

  • →

    It only works in your terminal. Pi is great for coding, but it doesn't work in your browser, on Telegram, or send notifications to your phone.

  • →

    You have to build everything yourself. Pi doesn't include memory, scheduling, or to-do lists — you have to write those features yourself.

  • →

    No built-in safety. Pi runs with whatever permissions you give it. You have to set up security yourself.

  • →

    OpenLumara has those features. Memory, scheduler, code helper, lists — all built in, all optional, all toggleable with one command.

Hermes

Why not Hermes?

  • →

    Local works — but slowly. Hermes supports local models, but some features can make them slow. OpenLumara is designed to keep local models fast.

  • →

It has a complicated onboarding wizard that was made for developers, and the default included skills are a mishmash of random stuff you might never need, including skills for training your own models. OpenLumara: clone, run, open the webUI, set your api details, done.

    Built for businesses. Hermes targets founders, agencies, and support teams. OpenLumara is built for your life: routines, habits, memory, reminders.

Customize

Make it uniquely yours.

OpenLumara comes with everything you need out of the box. But if you want to go further, you can add new features — whether you're a developer or not. Even your AI can help you build new features from scratch.

How a simple module looks 
import core

class ExampleModule(core.module.Module):
    '''
    A simple module that tracks how many times
    the user has been greeted.
    '''

    async def on_ready(self):
        self.count = 0

    async def on_user_message(self, content: str):
        if 'hello' in content.lower():
            self.count += 1
            return True

    async def greeting_count(self):
        '''Returns how many times the user said hello.'''
        return self.result(f'You said hello {self.count} times!', success=True)
